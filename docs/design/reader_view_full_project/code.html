Chapter Fourteen

The Silent Architect

auto_stories

The fog didn't roll into the valley so much as it materialized from the very stones of the cabin. Elias watched through the glass—bubbles of moisture clinging to the pane like unsaid thoughts. He hadn't touched the typewriter in three days. The ink ribbon had dried, its black tongue silenced by the weight of a story that refused to be told.

In the distance, the skeletal remains of the timber mill stood as a testament to an era of industry that the mountain had long since reclaimed. It was beautiful in its decay, a jagged silhouette against the bruised purple of the approaching dusk. He wondered if his own legacy would look much different—a structural skeleton stripped of its purpose, left to the mercy of the elements and the moss.

"You're overthinking the metaphor," a voice drifted from the corner of the room. It wasn't a voice of air and vibration, but a resonance within his own mind. Annie was there, or rather, the idea of her was—a shimmering presence near the bookshelf that held his father's collection of architectural blueprints.

He didn't turn around. To turn would be to acknowledge the hallucination, and Elias wasn't ready to be that lonely yet. "The mill is just a mill," he whispered, his breath fogging the glass further.

"No," Annie replied, her tone sharpening with that intellectual rigor he both feared and craved. "The mill is the architect's failure to account for time. You are trying to build a story that lasts forever, but stories are organic. They rot, Elias. Let this one rot a little. The soil will be better for it."

He finally turned. The room was empty, save for the long shadows stretching across the wide-planked floor. But on the desk, the carriage of the typewriter had moved. Just one space. A mechanical gasp in the silence of the woods.
